# Terra Verde — Marketing Campaign Brief (Phased: Free → Google)

**Prepared:** 2026-08-23 · revised 2026-08-23 — two-phase plan, no competitor-naming content
**Goal:** Drive free-tier signups (ongoing/evergreen motion)
**Structure:** **Phase 1 (now): free/organic channels only.** **Phase 2 (once Phase 1 is running): add a small Google Search Ads test.** No Facebook/Instagram ads for now.

---

## 1. Campaign Overview

**Campaign name:** "Start Free, Stay Free" (working title)

**One-sentence summary:** Get lawn-care operators currently on spreadsheets, paper, or nothing at all to try Terra Verde's genuinely permanent free tier — first through community presence and content (zero cost), then layer in Google Search Ads once that foundation is running.

**Primary objective:** 100 net-new free-tier signups within 90 days, with ≥15% converting to a paid tier within 60 days of signup. (Directional target — recalibrate once you know your actual baseline signup rate.)

**Secondary objectives:**
- Seed initial reviews on Capterra/G2/GetApp (target: 10+ reviews, 4.5★+ average, within 90 days) — Terra Verde currently has zero public review presence, a real credibility gap against established competitors
- Rank on page 1 for "free lawn care software" and similar generic (non-competitor-naming) terms within 6 months
- Build a repeatable content + community motion in Phase 1 that Phase 2's paid spend can amplify rather than replace

---

## 2. Target Audience

**Primary segment:** Solo lawn-care operators and small crews (1–10 employees) running their business on paper, spreadsheets, texts/calls, or a tool they've outgrown. Price-sensitive, time-poor, wary of software that hides pricing behind a sales call.

> A solo mow-and-blow operator or 2–5-person crew owner losing track of who's paid, who's due for service, and how to route the day efficiently — currently on a whiteboard, a spreadsheet, or a tool that's starting to feel limiting. They discover new tools through Facebook lawn-care groups, YouTube, LawnSite forum, and Google searches like "free lawn care software" or "lawn care scheduling app." They care most about: not getting nickel-and-dimed, not needing a card to try it, and not losing a day to onboarding.

**Secondary segment:** People actively searching with clear buying intent ("lawn care software," "free lawn care app") — smaller in volume, higher intent. This is exactly who Phase 2's Google Search Ads target.

**Buying stage alignment:** Primary segment is awareness→consideration. Secondary segment is consideration→decision.

---

## 3. Key Messages

**Core message:** Terra Verde is a genuinely free way to run your lawn-care business — real scheduling, routing, and billing, no card required, no countdown.

**Supporting messages:**

1. **"Free means free."** No credit card to start, no trial clock ticking down — a real working tier, not a 14-day taste.
2. **"You don't pay for every hand that touches a job."** No-login scheduling-only crew members — run a real crew without buying a paid seat for every mower operator.
3. **"Built for how weather actually runs your week."** Weather-integrated scheduling, right in the app — easy to demo in a screenshot or 15-second video.
4. **"Smarter routes, not just a map."** Real route optimization under the hood. Translate to outcomes ("fewer miles, more stops per day"), not the algorithm name.

**Message by channel:**
- Blog/SEO content: message #1 leads — this is what "free lawn care software" search intent is already asking about
- Community (Facebook groups, LawnSite, Reddit): message #2 and #3 lead — "did you know" discovery moments, not pitches
- Free-tier nurture email: message #4 and upgrade-path framing (Section 6)
- Phase 2 Google Ads: message #1 leads in ad copy — it directly answers the search query

**Honesty check:** do NOT claim live SMS, a customer self-service portal, online booking for existing customers, or QuickBooks sync unless confirmed live. A marketing claim contradicted at first login is worse than not claiming it, especially in a niche market where word-of-mouth runs both ways.

## 4. Channel Strategy

### Phase 1 — Free/organic (start immediately)

| Channel | Why it fits | Format | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Facebook lawn-care business groups** | This is where the primary audience actually lives and asks "what software do you use" organically | Participate genuinely (answer questions, help), don't just drop links — soft-mention Terra Verde when directly relevant | Medium, ongoing | **Fastest realistic path to signups** — real-time, trust-based, no ranking wait |
| **LawnSite forum** | Same audience, more serious/established operators | Same participate-genuinely approach; a well-answered thread has a long organic search tail | Medium, ongoing | |
| **Reddit** (r/lawncare, r/landscaping, small-business subs) | Lower trust for direct promotion, higher for organic mentions when genuinely the right answer | Answer questions, avoid anything that reads as an ad | Low-Medium | This audience punishes obvious self-promotion — be careful |
| **Review site seeding (Capterra, G2, GetApp)** | Zero review presence is a visible credibility gap the moment anyone compares options | Create free vendor profiles, then directly ask your first 15–20 free/paid users for a review | Low setup + ongoing ask | Start immediately — review count compounds over time |
| **Blog / SEO content** ("free lawn care software," "lawn care scheduling app," "how to route lawn crews efficiently") | Owns top-of-funnel search for the not-yet-shopping segment; also becomes the landing page Phase 2 ads point to | 1 post every 1–2 weeks, practical/how-to, not just pitches | Medium | Slow to rank (3–6+ months) — treat as the long-term compounding layer, not the near-term signup driver |
| **Email nurture for free-tier signups** | You already have the audience once they sign up | 4–6 email sequence: welcome → feature highlight → upgrade nudge near tier caps | Low-Medium | Ties directly to the conversion secondary objective |

**Phase 1 exit criteria** (when to move to Phase 2): community participation is running consistently for a few weeks, you have at least a couple of live blog posts up as ad-landing candidates, and you have a rough sense of your organic signup rate to compare paid performance against.

### Phase 2 — Add Google Search Ads (once Phase 1 is running)

Small test budget (~$5–10/day to start) on a **tightly-scoped keyword list** — this niche needs strict targeting or budget burns fast on irrelevant clicks (e.g., homeowners searching "lawn care" for a mowing service, not software).

- **Target keywords (software-intent only):** "lawn care software," "free lawn care software," "lawn care scheduling app," "lawn care business app," "lawn care invoicing software," "lawn crew route planning software"
- **Negative keywords (exclude consumer intent):** "lawn care near me," "lawn mowing service," "hire lawn care," "lawn care jobs," "lawn care company," "lawn treatment"
- **Landing page:** point ads at a dedicated `/free` or similar signup-focused page (or the best-performing blog post from Phase 1), not the generic homepage — message #1 ("free means free") should be the first thing visible above the fold
- **Why this order works:** Phase 1 builds the trust/content foundation and tells you which message actually lands; Phase 2 spends money confirming and scaling what Phase 1 already validated for free, instead of guessing blind with ad dollars first

### Not in this plan (for now)
- **Facebook/Instagram ads** — explicitly deferred per your call. Revisit as a Phase 3 once Google Search Ads performance is known — Facebook would work best as *retargeting* people who visited from Google/organic but didn't sign up, not as a cold top-of-funnel channel.
- **Comparison/"vs. [competitor]" content** — explicitly deferred, no competitor naming for now.

**Phase 2: small test budget, ~$150–300/mo (~$5–10/day)** once Phase 1 exit criteria are met. Keep it small and tightly scoped until you have real cost-per-signup data; scale only after that number looks sustainable against your free→paid conversion rate.

## 9. Risks and Mitigations

1. **Risk: Community self-promotion backfires.** Facebook groups and especially Reddit punish obvious vendor pitching.
   *Mitigation:* Weeks of genuine, helpful participation before any product mention; disclose you built it when you do mention it.

2. **Risk: Content claims something the product doesn't fully deliver yet** (SMS, customer portal, online booking, QuickBooks).
   *Mitigation:* Audit every piece against the actual live feature set, not the roadmap. Update the moment a gap closes.

3. **Risk: Zero review presence looks worse than a mediocre one during the seeding window.**
   *Mitigation:* Start review-seeding in week 1 — the earlier it starts, the shorter that awkward window lasts.

4. **Risk: Google Ads (Phase 2) burns budget on consumer "hire a lawn service" clicks instead of software buyers.**
   *Mitigation:* Strict negative-keyword list from day one of the test (see Section 4); review search-term report weekly for the first month and add negatives aggressively.
